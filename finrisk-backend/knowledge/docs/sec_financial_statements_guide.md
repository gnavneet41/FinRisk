# SEC Financial Statements Guide (FinRisk summary)

Summary written for FinRisk based on the U.S. SEC's "Beginners' Guide to Financial Statements" (SEC Office of Investor Education and Advocacy). Consult the official SEC publication for the full text.

## The four main statements

Public companies in the United States report a balance sheet, an income statement, a statement of cash flows and a statement of shareholders' equity. The annual report filed with the SEC is the Form 10-K; quarterly reports are filed on Form 10-Q.

## Balance sheet

The balance sheet shows what a company owns (assets), what it owes (liabilities) and what is left for shareholders (shareholders' equity) at one point in time. It always balances: assets = liabilities + shareholders' equity. Current assets and current liabilities are those expected to be turned into cash or settled within one year.

## Income statement

The income statement reports revenue and expenses over a period and the resulting net income or net loss. Typical lines are net sales (revenue), cost of sales, gross profit, operating expenses, operating income, income before taxes, income taxes and net income, followed by earnings per share.

## Statement of cash flows

The statement of cash flows explains the change in cash over the period in three groups: operating activities (cash from the business itself), investing activities (for example buying or selling property, equipment or securities) and financing activities (for example borrowing, repaying debt, paying dividends or buying back shares). A company can report net income while generating little cash, which is why this statement is read together with the income statement.

## Notes and management discussion

The notes to the financial statements explain accounting policies and give detail behind the numbers. The Management's Discussion and Analysis (MD&A) section of the 10-K explains results, liquidity and capital resources in management's own words.
